How a page view becomes a price

Every time you load a web page, dozens of companies receive information about you. This is how that data is collected, matched together, and used to decide what you pay.

Three simulations · built from documented mechanisms Updated October 2026 Reading time 12 minutes

Collection

One page load, in real time

The following simulation shows what happens in the first second after a page loads. It is built from documented mechanisms in the FTC's tracking enforcement and the OpenRTB specification. The counts are representative of a typical ad-supported site.

Scenario

Step 1 of 7

The browser asks for the page

Your browser sends the site a request. That request includes your IP address, your browser and operating system, your screen size, and any cookie the site has already stored.

This is the request you intended to make.

1 request · 1 company
Step 2 of 7

A single script tag loads a tag manager

One line of HTML. The tag manager's only job is to inject other companies' code into the page.

One line becomes a dozen requests.

+1 request · +1 company
Step 3 of 7

Analytics and a social pixel

Analytics assigns a persistent client ID that follows you across visits. The social pixel sets a cross-site cookie so the same company recognizes you on every site that carries it.

If you are signed in, that pixel can send a hash of your email address — a link to your real identity, and to every other site where you have used that address.

+2 requests · +2 companies
Step 4 of 7

A wrapper packages the page for auction

Header bidding assembles the bid request: the page, the ad slots, the consent string, the client ID, and any audience segments attached to it.

This is what gets broadcast.

+1 request
Step 5 of 7

Three exchanges fan the request out to bidders

Each exchange uses a standard called OpenRTB. Bidders have milliseconds to decide what you are worth.

One wins and shows an ad. The rest keep the data.

+3 requests · +35 bidders
Step 6 of 7

The winning ad loads its own trackers

The creative arrives with measurement scripts attached: viewability, verification, attribution. Each one is a separate company.

+1 request · +3 companies
Step 7 of 7

And identifiers that survive a cookie wipe

Two scripts do not rely on cookies. Canvas, font and audio fingerprinting identify the browser itself. A data broker beacon writes the visit into a profile the broker will license later.

+2 requests · +2 companies

Pricing

How a profile sets a price

Every pricing model asks the same question: at each price, how likely is this person to buy? With no data it can only use the average customer. With a profile it estimates the individual's willingness to pay, and the revenue-maximizing price moves toward that estimate. Drag the sliders to see the model respond.
