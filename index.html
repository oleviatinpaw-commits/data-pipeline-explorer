01 · Collection

One page load, in real time

01
Scenario

Illustrative model of a typical ad-supported site. Request timing and counts are representative, not a measurement of any one site.

Step 1

You open the page

Your browser asks the site for the article. The server sends back HTML, and that first-party request carries what it needs to render: your IP, your browser, your screen size, and any cookie the site already set.

This is the only request you intended to make.

1 request · 1 company
Step 2

One line of that HTML loads a tag manager

A single <script> tag. Its only job is to inject other companies' code into the page.

One line becomes a dozen requests.

+1 request · +1 company
Step 3

Analytics and a social pixel

Analytics assigns you a persistent client ID that follows you across visits. The social pixel sets a cross-site cookie so the same company recognizes you on every site that carries it.

And if you're logged in here, the pixel can send a hash of your email address — which links this visit to your real identity, and to every other site where you used that address.

+2 requests · +2 companies
Step 4

A wrapper packages everything known about you

Header bidding builds the bid request: the page, the ad slots, your consent string, your client ID, and every audience segment anyone has attached to it.

It is now a product description, and you are the product.

+1 request
Step 5

Three exchanges broadcast it to every bidder

Each exchange fans the request out using OpenRTB. Bidders have milliseconds to decide what you're worth.

One wins and shows an ad. Every other bidder keeps the data. The losing bids are not returned.

+3 requests · +35 bidders
Step 6

The winning ad loads its own trackers

The creative arrives with measurement scripts attached: viewability, verification, attribution. Each one is another company.

None of them asked you.

+1 request · +3 companies
Step 7

And then the identifiers that survive a cookie wipe

Canvas, font and audio fingerprinting recognize this browser after you clear cookies. A data broker beacon writes this page into a profile it will license later.

Nothing you saw on the page mentioned either one.

03 · Monetization

How an algorithm turns the profile into your price

Every pricing model asks the same question: at each price, how likely is this person to say yes? With no data it can only use the average customer, so everyone gets the list price. With a profile it estimates your curve, and the revenue-maximizing price moves toward the most you would tolerate. Drag the sliders.
